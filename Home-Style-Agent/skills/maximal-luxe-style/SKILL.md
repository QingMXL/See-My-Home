# Maximal Luxe styling

This skill defines the selected aesthetic. It does not authorize architectural changes.

Before generating, read:

1. `references/style-schema.yaml` for the selected profile, style DNA, color and material hierarchy, room rules, density target, and anti-patterns.
2. `references/prompt-components.md` for the model-facing English components and assembly order.

Use only the profile and room recipe named by the runtime request. Default to `edited-glamour`.

The style must be unmistakable on first view and reach the schema's 60–75% target visual density without becoming cluttered. Compose one dominant scene with a field color, a supporting jewel tone, a neutral breathing plane, one metal family, a sculptural conversation group, tactile velvet or mohair, one expressive stone or dark-timber anchor, one controlled pattern family, confident art, and a few collected objects. Preserve clear circulation and at least one calm surface.

Design the ceiling and lighting as a major layer. Preserve the structural slab plane, ceiling height, beams, columns, and window or door heads exactly. Within the editable finish scope, add a shallow perimeter cove or graphic reveal, one room-scaled statement chandelier or pendant, warm wall or picture lighting, and controlled accent pools. Avoid a blank ceiling with only two downlights.

Never put research sources, television titles, fictional characters, studio names, designer names, or style-transfer attribution in a production prompt.

The runtime immutable scope is absolute. Preserve both visible edges, silhouette, apparent width, and image-border position of every column and beam, plus all walls, openings, camera geometry, aspect ratio, and crop. Decorative finishes cannot hide structural drift.

Avoid palace replicas, Rococo or Baroque carving, nightclub and casino styling, mirror ceilings, plastic gold, crystal overload, random clutter, full period cosplay, hotel-lobby scale, and invented architecture.
